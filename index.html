Tables are extracted from the PDF's actual drawn grid lines when present (the same approach professional table-extraction tools use). Running headers/footers and section markers are scrubbed from cell text piece-by-piece, so they're caught even when split across two cells by imperfect column detection — without touching legitimate short cells or roman-numeral markers like "(i)"/"(ii)" that appear in real data. If a "new row"'s serial number isn't a plausible next value (e.g. a stray page-footer number bleeding into that column), it's treated as a continuation of the previous row instead of a bogus new entry. A "Notes:" heading, or the lettered notes list itself, marks the definite end of the table. Pages without visible grid lines fall back to text-position clustering.
